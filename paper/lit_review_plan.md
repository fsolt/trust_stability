# Literature Review — writing plan

Spine: **the discontent-destabilizes claim is stated generally but has been tested almost
only on democracies; where it is tested with autocracies included, the dependent variable
is a democracy score, never a coded regime event.**

Target length ~1,600–1,800 words, six subsections. Boundaries: the Theory section already
covers Easton/Lipset/Gurr/Kuran/Svolik/O'Donnell and the discontent-vs-democratic-support
distinction; the Data section already covers the conceptualization of discontent, the
procedural-vs-actor-centered regime debate, and transitologist-vs-incrementalist. **Do not
re-argue any of those.** This section does one thing the others do not: it establishes that
the empirical question has not been answered, and why.

---

## Phrasing of the gap — use this, not a stronger version

> Existing cross-national tests of mass attitudes and regime change are almost exclusively
> about democracies, and the few that include autocracies use continuous democracy scores
> rather than coded regime events. Whether public discontent predicts the onset of regime
> change in autocracies has not been tested.

Do **not** write "the autocratic attitude literature is severely under-developed." It is not
— the attitude literature on autocracies is large. What is missing is the *dependent
variable*. Overclaiming here is the easiest way to lose a referee who works on authoritarian
public opinion.

---

## §1. A general claim, a narrow archive (~300 words)

Move: the intuition that lost legitimacy destabilizes regimes is stated without regime
qualification, but its entire empirical archive is democracies.

- The critical-citizens / satisfaction-with-democracy / deconsolidation literature is built
  on consolidated democracies: Norris 2011, Dalton 2004, Foa & Mounk 2016/2017 and the
  rebuttals (Alexander & Welzel 2017; Voeten 2017; Wuttke, Gavras & Schoen 2022).
- **Anchor citation for the asymmetry:** Valgarðsson et al. 2025 (*BJPS* 55:e15), 3,377
  surveys, 143 countries, 1958–2019 — *explicitly excludes autocracies* from its regional
  and global trend analyses. This is the single strongest sentence-level evidence that the
  field's own flagship descriptive work stops at the regime boundary.
- Close: the general claim has never faced the cases where the theory says it should bite
  hardest. §2 explains that this is not oversight.

## §2. Why the autocratic cell stayed empty — and why the objection is weaker than assumed (~400 words)

Move: name the measurement objection, concede its force, then show it cuts in the paper's
favour. **This paragraph converts the paper's biggest vulnerability into a design virtue and
must not be buried in a footnote or the appendix.**

- The theoretical reason: preference falsification (Kuran 1991, already in Theory — reference
  it, do not re-explain).
- The documented magnitude: Robinson & Tannenberg 2019 (*Research & Politics*); Tannenberg
  2022 (37 African countries, ~20 percentage-point inflation of regime support); Jiang & Yang
  2016 (*Comparative Political Studies* 49(5):600–634 — note the journal, it is miscited as
  JLEO in several places); Carter et al. 2024 (*China Quarterly*).
- The field's own retreat, quotable: the authoritarian-legitimacy literature deliberately
  studies regime *claims* rather than citizen beliefs because autocratic legitimacy is
  "notoriously difficult to measure with public opinion surveys" (Tannenberg et al. 2021,
  *EPSR*; Grauvogel & von Soest, Oxford Handbook 2024). Hence Gerschewski 2013's three
  pillars, von Soest & Grauvogel 2017, Dukalskis & Gerschewski 2017 — all claim-side.
- The limits of the objection: Shen & Truex 2021 (*BJPS*) find near-null self-censorship in
  most autocracies, China the outlier; Frye et al. show the list-experiment corrective is
  itself unreliable; **no published work argues that autocratic regime-support measures are
  flatly uninterpretable.**
- The payoff sentence: every documented bias runs *pro-regime*, so it attenuates any
  discontent coefficient toward zero. Our autocracy estimate is therefore conservative — the
  measurement problem cannot manufacture our result, only suppress it.
- Flag for the authors: **the manuscript currently says nothing about any of this.** This is
  a required addition, not an optional one.

## §3. What has been tested, sorted by dependent variable (~450 words)

Move: sort the literature by DV rather than by IV. The gap becomes arithmetic rather than
rhetorical.

| DV type | Representative work | What it can and cannot show |
|---|---|---|
| Attitudes / participation | Norris 2011; Dalton 2004; Foa & Mounk; Torcal & Montero 2006 | no regime outcome enters at all |
| Democracy *score* | Claassen 2020 (*AJPS*); Welzel 2007; Inglehart & Welzel 2005; Qi & Shin 2011 | continuous level, not an event; and see below |
| Coded regime *event* | Przeworski & Limongi 1997; Boix & Stokes 2003; Svolik 2008; Houle 2009; Gasiorowski 1995; Geddes et al. 2014; Gassebner et al. 2013 | mass opinion never appears on the right-hand side |

Two sentences to write carefully:

1. **The nearest existing test is a null with no power.** Claassen 2020's split sample is
   significant in democracies and insignificant in autocracies; the 2024 erratum reports the
   autocracy long-run effect as 4.40, 95% CI [−23.00, 25.23]. That interval is not evidence
   of no effect; it is evidence that the question has not been answered. Say this explicitly
   — it is the most efficient single demonstration that the cell is empty.
2. **Concede the design precedent honestly and early.** Sato & Wiebrecht 2024 (*PRQ*
   77(3):1010–1025) already uses ERT episode-onset as a binary DV, splits by regime type,
   and covers 148 countries 2000–2022 with probit and IV, with an expert-coded
   *disinformation* IV. The apparatus is therefore not novel; the independent variable is.
   Cite it as a precedent that legitimates the design rather than letting a referee produce
   it as a rival. **This is the paper's main novelty exposure — handle it in the text, not
   in a response letter.**

## §4. The behavioral detour, and why it cannot settle direction (~350 words)

Move: the only route that consistently delivers macro effects measures behavior, not
attitudes — and it does not agree with itself about direction.

- The optimistic camp: Teorell 2010; Chenoweth & Stephan 2011; Celestino & Gleditsch 2013;
  Brancati 2016; Kadivar 2018.
- The dissenting camp (three studies, direct evidence): Ulfelder 2005 (*IPSR* 26(3):311–334 —
  the effect of contentious action on autocratic breakdown "varies in strength and
  direction"); Hellmeier & Bernhard 2023 (*CPS* 56(12):1858–1890, mobilization for democracy
  *and* autocracy, 1900–2021); Chin, Song & Wright 2023 (*BJPS* 53(1):25–44).
  **Do not include Timoneda et al. 2023 here** — its IV is failed coups, an elite challenge,
  with no mobilization variable; usable only as a mechanism analogy.
- The unexamined discontinuity: Chenoweth 2020 (*JoD* 31(3):69–84) reports nonviolent-campaign
  success falling from ~65% in the 1990s to under 34% after 2010, and **nobody has re-run the
  optimistic models on the post-2010 sample.**
- Add Davenport 2007's punishment puzzle as the repression-side analogue.
- Close by setting up H3: a literature that cannot agree on direction is exactly what one
  should expect if discontent opens a moment of contingency without determining how it
  resolves. This makes finding (b) — onset yes, direction no — read as theory-confirming
  rather than as a failed test.

## §5. Whether the answer depends on how regime change is coded (~250 words)

Move: pre-empt the finding-(c) exposure by making coding disagreement a known problem of the
field rather than an anomaly of this paper.

- Casper & Tufis 2003 (*Political Analysis*) → Cheibub, Gandhi & Vreeland 2010 (*Public
  Choice*) → Vaccaro 2021 (*EPS* 20(4):666–684: interchangeability is weak **and has been
  declining since the 1980s**) → the Little & Meng / Knutsen et al. exchange in *PS*
  57(2).
- The point that earns the paper something: **this entire dispute has been conducted at the
  level of index correlations.** Table 4's event-level overlap analysis is original evidence
  in it.
- Caveat to state plainly: Vaccaro's remedy for weak interchangeability is validating across
  measures. This paper validates across measures and then prefers one. Say why (the ERT
  framework's episode logic matches the theoretical object) rather than leaving the choice
  implicit.

## §6. What this paper does (~200 words)

Four-point positioning, then hand off to Theory:
1. attitudes, not protest behavior;
2. diffuse discontent with the system one lives under, not support for democratic principles;
3. coded regime events with autocracies included, not democracy scores or democratic-country
   attitude trends;
4. measurement uncertainty propagated, which Tai, Hu & Solt 2024 shows can reverse
   conclusions in exactly this literature.

Last sentence must set up, not answer, the Theory section's opening question ("Why should the
distribution of attitudes across a population bear on the survival of a political regime?").

## Changes this recon forces elsewhere in the manuscript

1. **H1 is written more generally than the results support.** Lührmann & Lindberg 2019 (~68%
   of autocratization episodes are incumbent-led), Bermeo 2016, and Wuttke et al. 2022
   together make a serious elite-led-backsliding challenge. The results support a
   *regime-conditional* claim only. Either narrow H1 or add an explicit paragraph conceding
   that mass discontent is one input among elite-driven ones.
2. **Add the attenuation argument** (§2 above) to the manuscript. Currently absent.
3. **Cite Sato & Wiebrecht 2024** in Data/Measurement as the ERT-onset design precedent.
4. **Acknowledge the Foa & Mounk gap**: their early-warning claim is about the *trend* in
   support; this paper tests *levels*. Belongs in Discussion, one sentence.
5. **Consider a model change to reconcile findings (b) and (c):** condition DD/BMR transitions
   on ERT episode status. This would turn an awkwardness into a consistency result. Analysis
   decision, not a writing decision — flagged for the authors.
6. **Fix the citation keys.** All 51 citations in the qmd are unresolved against the re-keyed
   main.bib. See `scratchpad/citekey_remap.txt`.
